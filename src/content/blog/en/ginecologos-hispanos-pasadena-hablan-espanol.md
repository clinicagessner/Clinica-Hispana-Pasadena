Many women go years without a gynecological checkup not for lack of time, but out of embarrassment at explaining something intimate in another language or to someone who does not understand them. In Pasadena it can be handled in Spanish or English and with no appointment. This guide walks through the visit step by step to take the mystery out of it.

## What is a gynecology visit in Spanish like?

It starts with a conversation, not with the exam. The medical team asks about your cycle, your pregnancies, the birth control you use, whether you have any discomfort and when your last Pap smear was. You can bring someone you trust if it makes you feel safer. Only afterward, and if needed, comes the physical exam, which is explained before it is done: what will be checked, what you will feel and how long it takes. You have the right to ask to stop at any time.

## What does the exam include, and does it hurt?

The pelvic exam takes a few minutes. The outer area is checked, then a speculum is placed to see the cervix and, if due, the Pap sample is taken with a soft brush; you may feel pressure or brief discomfort, not pain. If there is discharge or symptoms, a culture is taken at the same time. The breast exam is optional and is done if you have a lump or pain. Nothing is done without explaining it to you.

## What can I ask without embarrassment?

Anything. The most frequent questions in the exam room: why sex hurts, whether a discharge is normal, which contraceptive fits if I do not want hormones, why my period is late, whether bleeding between periods is serious, what to do about hot flashes in menopause, and whether I can get pregnant after removing the implant. None of them is unusual. Write them in your phone before coming so you do not forget.

## Is it confidential?

Yes. What is discussed and the results are yours. They are not shared with your partner or family without your permission, and no immigration status is requested to see you. Sexually transmitted infection tests are done and treated with the same privacy.

## When is the first visit, and what happens in the teen years?

The first gynecology visit can be between ages 13 and 15, and it is almost always just a conversation: about the cycle, hygiene, the HPV vaccine and the questions of that age. A Pap smear is not done before 21. A teenager can come with her mother and ask for a moment alone with the medical team; both are normal.

## What can be handled at the same visit?

Pap smear, cultures, pregnancy test, testing and treatment of vaginal and urinary infections, starting or switching birth control, removal of the subdermal implant and pelvic ultrasound.
