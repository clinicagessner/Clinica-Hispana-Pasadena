Many men only see a doctor when the pain won't let them work anymore. The problem is that the most dangerous conditions — high blood pressure, diabetes, prostate problems — advance silently for years. A one-hour preventive checkup can catch them early, while they are still simple to treat.

## Why should men get checkups?

Work, family, lack of time: there is always a reason to put off the visit. But an annual checkup lets you:

- **Catch high blood pressure, diabetes and high cholesterol early**, before they damage your heart or kidneys.
- **Monitor your prostate** starting at a certain age, when the risk increases.
- **Check your testosterone**, which affects energy, mood and sexual health.
- **Avoid costly emergencies**: treating a condition early is always simpler than treating a complication.

## Which exams do you need at your age?

| Age | Recommended exams | Frequency |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 18–39 | General checkup, blood pressure, glucose and cholesterol; STI testing depending on your situation | Annual checkup; glucose and cholesterol every 3–5 years (sooner with risk factors) |
| 40–49 | All of the above + full lipid panel; discuss PSA if you have a family history of prostate cancer | Annual |
| 50–64 | All of the above + PSA and prostate check; electrocardiogram if you have risk factors | Annual |
| 65+ | Full checkup, PSA, heart, kidney and medication review | Annual or as your doctor advises |

Every case is different: at your visit we review your history and tell you exactly which tests you need.

## Warning signs: don't ignore them

See a doctor as soon as possible if you notice:

- Chest pain or pressure, especially with exertion
- Difficulty, burning or blood when urinating
- Excessive thirst, frequent urination or unexplained weight loss
- Frequent headaches or blurry vision
- Swelling in your legs or ankles
- Changes in sexual function
- Lumps or changes in the testicles

## Practical tips between checkups

- Walk or get physical activity at least 30 minutes, 5 days a week.
- Cut back on sodas, alcohol and fried food; drink more water.
- Sleep 7 to 8 hours: poor sleep raises blood pressure and blood sugar.
- If you smoke, ask for help quitting; the benefits start on day one.
- Don't ignore stress: it also affects your heart and blood pressure.
