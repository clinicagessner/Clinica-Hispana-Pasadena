You've talked it over at the kitchen table and decided you'd like a baby next year. What almost nobody does at that point is the thing that helps most: getting checked before stopping birth control. Plenty of women show up at their first pregnancy visit with high blood pressure they didn't know about, with anemia, or with a slow thyroid. All of that can be found, and usually corrected, a few months ahead.

That visit is called a preconception checkup, and all it takes is having pregnancy in your plans. Here's what gets checked, what you can start at home and what to do the day the test comes back positive.

## Why get checked before, not after

A baby's organs start forming in the first weeks, often before you even know you're pregnant. Your sugar, your blood pressure, your medicines and cigarettes matter from the start. On top of that, certain vaccines aren't given during pregnancy, and some treatments for chronic conditions are better changed calmly, not overnight.

## What the preconception checkup looks at

The visit starts with a conversation with the medical team at Clínica Hispana Nueva Salud Pasadena: your cycles, past pregnancies, any losses, family illnesses, what you take every day and when you had your last Pap smear. Bring your medicine bottles and, if you have them, earlier lab results to compare.

Then your blood pressure and weight are taken, and the team decides which tests make sense for you. This table sums up the usual ones:

| What is checked | Why it matters before pregnancy |
| --- | --- |
| Blood pressure | Uncontrolled high blood pressure complicates pregnancy and often has no symptoms. |
| Glucose and A1C | They show hidden diabetes or prediabetes; high sugar early on affects how the baby forms. |
| Complete blood count | Picks up anemia, common in women with heavy periods. |
| Thyroid (TSH) | A thyroid working too little or too much can make pregnancy harder and is best adjusted first. |
| Kidney, liver and cholesterol | They set a baseline if you already have a chronic condition or take medicine. |
| Pap smear | If you're due for one at your age, it's better done before pregnancy than during it. |
| Vaccine record | To see what's missing and catch up in time. |

The blood draw happens at the clinic under [blood tests](/en/services/examenes-sangre), and the medical team walks you through each number in Spanish or English once the result is in. For fasting glucose and the lipid panel, come in the morning before eating; thyroid and blood count tests don't need fasting.

The Pap smear, along with the HPV test when your age calls for it, is done during the [gynecology](/en/services/ginecologia) visit. If you have pelvic pain, very irregular periods or bleeding between periods, a pelvic ultrasound can be added at the same clinic to look at the uterus and ovaries before you start trying.

## Living with diabetes, high blood pressure or thyroid trouble

None of these rule out pregnancy, but they are a reason to plan with more lead time: the goal is to arrive with glucose and blood pressure on target and a stable thyroid. Diabetes, blood pressure and cholesterol follow-up is handled under [chronic conditions](/en/services/condiciones-cronicas).

Some medicines that work well for you today aren't the best choice in pregnancy. Don't drop any of them on your own, because stopping suddenly carries its own risk: tell the medical team your plans and together you'll go over which to keep and which to switch. If your thyroid or blood sugar proves hard to control, a specialized evaluation before trying is a good idea.

## Vaccines: what to look for in your record

The clinic's [vaccines](/en/services/vacunas) page lists the flu shot and tetanus toxoid (Td or Tdap) for adults, plus measles, mumps and rubella (MMR), chickenpox (varicella), hepatitis B and pneumococcal vaccines, given mainly when they're missing from the record for the immigration exam. What matters for your plans:

- **MMR and varicella are not given during pregnancy.** If you have no proof you received them, now is the time to bring it up, because afterward you'll have to wait. Call ahead to ask whether the vaccine you're missing is available; the medical team will tell you how long to wait before trying to conceive.
- **The flu shot is given in pregnancy** and is recommended for pregnant women. Getting it during flu season protects you before and after you conceive.
- **Tdap is recommended in every pregnancy**, on top of the Td booster adults need every 10 years.

Bring your vaccine card, whatever language it's in.

## What you can start at home today

- **Folic acid.** Every woman who could become pregnant is advised to take it daily from before she starts trying, because it helps prevent defects of the baby's spine and brain that form very early. The amount and form that suit you come from the medical team, especially if you had a pregnancy with that kind of problem or take medicine for seizures.
- **Quit smoking and vaping.** Tobacco is linked to miscarriage, low birth weight and early delivery. If your partner smokes, that counts too: smoke at home reaches you all the same.
- **Stop drinking.** No safe amount of alcohol in pregnancy is known, and since the first weeks go by before you know, the simplest move is to stop once you start trying.
- **Review what you take over the counter.** Teas, store supplements and weight-loss remedies count too: bring them to the visit.

## The day the test comes back positive

If a home test came back positive, or your period is late and you want to know for sure, come in for a [pregnancy test](/en/services/prueba-embarazo). The urine test is reliable from the first day of a missed period; the blood test picks up the hormone a few days earlier and, in the version that measures the amount, helps estimate how far along you are. Once the result is confirmed:

1. Your estimated due date is worked out from your last period.
2. Your history is reviewed: blood pressure, diabetes, medicines and past pregnancies.
3. Folic acid and prenatal vitamins are recommended from that day on, if you weren't taking them.
4. Initial lab work is ordered, if you'd like it.
5. An ultrasound is scheduled between weeks 6 and 8 to confirm the pregnancy is inside the uterus and see the heartbeat.

Whatever you checked beforehand saves steps now: the results are in and your medicines were already adjusted. For ongoing pregnancy care, the medical team explains what comes next.

## When not to wait: 911 or the emergency room

With a positive test, or if you think you might be pregnant, don't wait for a visit if you have:

- Severe pain on one side of the belly, or intense pelvic pain that came on suddenly.
- Vaginal bleeding, especially if it's heavy and comes with dizziness or fainting.
- A high temperature that shows up along with lower-belly pain.
- Vomiting that won't let you keep even water down.

One-sided pain with bleeding can signal a pregnancy outside the uterus, which is an emergency.

## Your list before stopping birth control

- Blood pressure, glucose, blood count and thyroid checked.
- Pap smear up to date for your age.
- Vaccine record reviewed, with MMR and varicella sorted out before trying.
- Daily folic acid started.
- Cigarettes and alcohol out of the routine.
- Diabetes, blood pressure or thyroid under control, without stopping anything on your own.
