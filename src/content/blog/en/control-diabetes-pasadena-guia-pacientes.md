Managing diabetes is not about giving up the food you love: it is about knowing your numbers, what moves them, and getting checked on time. This guide gathers the targets the medical team uses, the checks due every year and the signs that should not wait, so you manage your diabetes with data, not fear.

## What are the blood sugar targets for most adults?

- **A1C:** under 7%. It is the average blood sugar of the last three months and the number that best sums up control. It is measured with a blood test every 3 months while treatment is being adjusted, and every 6 months when stable.
- **Fasting glucose:** between 80 and 130 mg/dL.
- **Glucose two hours after a meal:** under 180 mg/dL.
- **Blood pressure:** under 130/80.
- **LDL cholesterol:** below 100, and lower if there has already been a heart problem.

Targets are adjusted for age and other conditions; the physician tells you yours. What matters is knowing them and writing down your readings to compare at each visit.

## Which checks should I have every year?

1. **Blood and urine tests:** A1C, cholesterol, kidney function (creatinine and urine albumin) and liver function.
2. **Foot exam:** sensation, pulses, skin and nails. A small unfelt wound can become an ulcer.
3. **Dilated eye exam:** retinopathy has no symptoms until it is advanced.
4. **Blood pressure and weight:** at every visit.
5. **Vaccines:** flu every season, pneumococcal and hepatitis B if you do not have them.
6. **Mouth check:** diabetes increases gum disease.

## Which signs should not wait?

Excessive thirst and urination with tiredness and blurred vision point to very high sugar. Shaking, cold sweat, confusion or sudden hunger point to low sugar: take something sugary right away and check. A foot wound that does not close in a week, pain or burning in the legs at night, or vomiting with abdominal pain call for a same-day visit.

## How do I eat without giving up home cooking?

Tortillas, rice and beans fit on the plate; the question is proportion. Practical rule: half the plate vegetables, a quarter protein (chicken, fish, egg, beans), a quarter carbohydrate (one or two tortillas, half a cup of rice). Swap sodas and juices for water or unsweetened fruit water. Eat at regular times without skipping meals, and walk 20 to 30 minutes after the main meal: it lowers sugar more than any supplement.

## Which medications are used, and when are they adjusted?

Metformin is usually the first medication and is adjusted based on A1C; others are added if it is not enough, and insulin when the pancreas no longer produces enough. None of them works if taken "when I remember." Always bring your bottles to the visit; if a medication does not agree with you, it is changed, not abandoned.
